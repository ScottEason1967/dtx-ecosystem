Assurance envelope: boundary between Commercial and IG/DPO

Plain-English statement of who owns what across the assurance envelope. The most load-bearing internal boundary in the interim model.

StatusActive
OwnerHomeTest Commercial lead and NHSE IG/DPO interface
Date taken28 May 2026
Review cadenceStanding joint forum (monthly during build, quarterly when settled)

Position

HomeTest Commercial owns the assurance templates, the supplier admission criteria and the contractual interface between NHSE and suppliers. NHSE IG/DPO owns the legal substance of the IG envelope, the controllership rulings, the lawful-basis interpretation and the final sign-off on any template or supplier admission. No template issues without IG/DPO sign-off. No envelope-fit ruling is final without IG/DPO authority. The boundary is stated so neither side colonises the other and so suppliers and commissioners see one coherent answer.

Rationale

The interim model places the assurance layer at the centre of NHSE's value-add. Without a clear boundary, two failure modes are likely. Commercial appears to colonise IG/DPO territory by curating templates and supplier criteria without legal authority, exposing NHSE to challenge from a supplier or the ICO. Or IG/DPO is treated as a rubber-stamp service rather than the legal authority, and Commercial routinely under-resources the IG sign-off step until a breach exposes the gap.

Stating the boundary plainly removes both risks. Commercial holds the operational machinery (templates, criteria, contracting interface) because that is where the commercial team has standing expertise. IG/DPO holds the legal substance because that is where the authority sits under UK GDPR and NHSE governance. Where they meet (envelope-fit rulings, template sign-off, breach assessment), IG/DPO has the final word.

Reopen triggers

  • NHSE IG/DPO function restructure changes the authority lines (delegation to a different team, devolution to programme level).
  • A specific assurance-envelope dispute requires the boundary to be sharpened further (a contested envelope-fit ruling reveals a gap).
  • Joint controllership architecture changes (for example, NHS App, HealthStore integration or a cross-platform data flow that requires fresh controllership designation).
  • A regulatory or ICO finding requires a different split of authority.
  • The MHRA 8 June 2026 ruling that the HomeTest platform is not a Software as a Medical Device removes one regulatory pathway from the boundary, the platform layer itself is out of MHRA scope; supplier kits and lab side remain in scope.
  • The IAO designation (9 June 2026) closes the IG sign-off precondition for live patient data processing; the operational responsibility now sits with the designated IAO under NHSE governance.

Dependencies

  • Standing joint forum operating. NHSE IG/DPO, HomeTest Commercial, Clinical Safety and rotating supplier IG leads. Monthly during build, quarterly when steady. Surfaces issues before they become incidents.
  • Co-chair pattern between Commercial lead and IG/DPO interface. Joint chairing keeps the boundary visible and prevents one side defaulting to running the forum.
  • Standing template library version-controlled by Commercial. DPIA template, DSA template, supplier IG criteria, controllership designation library. Each version released only after IG/DPO sign-off.
  • Clear escalation route to NHSE IG/DPO leadership for contested rulings. The first contested envelope-fit decision sets the precedent for what counts as a substantive disagreement.

Operating consequences

The boundary has practical consequences worth naming. Commercial does not author template changes unilaterally; updates go through IG/DPO sign-off before release. IG/DPO does not run supplier-facing operations (templates, application admin, contracting); those sit with Commercial. Where a supplier asks "does this fit the envelope", the Commercial lead answers the commercial half (does it fit the supplier criteria, the call-off shape, the operational rules) and IG/DPO answers the IG half (does it fit the lawful basis, the controllership map, the sub-processor rules). Both answers together are the ruling.
