HomeTest position papers

The formal positions HomeTest Commercial has adopted on the commercial model: the vehicle, the interim route, the lot, who runs it, the money, how suppliers get on and stay on, and how the team engages. Each paper crystallises a single decision, the rationale behind it, the triggers that would reopen it, and the dependencies it rests on. Each opens in a click and prints clean as a one-pager.

What these papers are. Position papers are short, declarative statements of the positions HomeTest Commercial has adopted. Everything commercial is agreed in principle and remains subject to the live procurement; each paper says what is settled, what is assumed and what would reopen it. They are designed to be read standalone, quoted in meetings and attached to emails without navigating the operating model or the playbook, and each links back to the plays and the model pages that carry the detail.

Paper 1In principle

The commercial vehicle: the Open Framework

An NHSE-enabled Open Framework under the Procurement Act 2023: one framework, one end-to-end lot, suppliers assured once, commissioners calling off locally below a national ceiling. Circa £250m over up to five years; go-live calendar Q1 2028 in principle.

Paper 2Live

The interim route: how commissioners buy until the framework lands

Existing frameworks and memoranda of understanding carry the April 2027 HIV rollout and the PSA pilot while the framework is built. Guidance to commissioners, not a new vehicle; runs to around January 2028 and retires into the framework.

Paper 3In principle

The lotting choice: one end-to-end lot

The framework buys the whole journey, request to result at home, as one lot. Capability lots were assessed and set aside; the rationale and the counter-arguments are on record, with conditions to test before the strategy is finalised.

Open paper
Paper 4In principle

Who runs the framework, and NHS England’s role

NHSE sets the standard, opens the gates, holds the NHS App and takes a defined share of the levy back. A framework provider, under live selection between external providers and NHS itself, runs the framework. NHSE holds no budget and touches no payment.

Open paper
Paper 5In principle

How the money works

Commissioners pay suppliers direct at a cost per activity below a national ceiling; suppliers pay the provider a levy of roughly one to two per cent; a defined share returns to NHSE and is reinvested. The return leg is the critical assumption until settled.

Open paper
Paper 6In principle

Supplier admission: assured once, then each test at its gate

Two-tier assurance: the company once through the Master SCAL, each test at its gate on a published roadmap. Assurance is what makes the framework worth joining; the commercial machinery sits on top of it.

Open paper
Paper 7In principle

Supplier performance and removal

Performance is held in the call-off through KPIs and commercial recourse; persistent or serious failure escalates to removal from the register under a written, appealable protocol. Two levers, not one.

Open paper
Paper 8Active

Commercial and IG/DPO: who owns what in assurance

Commercial owns the templates, the admission criteria and the contractual interface; IG/DPO owns the legal substance and the final word on envelope fit and sign-off. Stated so neither side colonises the other.

Paper 9Proposed · decision to take

Engagement: the pre-sales cell

Engagement runs through one integrated pre-sales cell under sponsor leadership, not a separate sales team. A proposal from the commercial team, not yet decided; it stays here because the question has to be answered before the machinery scales.
