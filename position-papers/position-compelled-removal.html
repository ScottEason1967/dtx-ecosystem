Supplier performance and removal

Performance is managed through the KPIs in the call-off, with commercial recourse in the contract. Persistent or serious failure escalates to removal from the accredited register. The protocol has to be defensible the first time it bites.

StatusAgreed in principle · protocol to be written
OwnerHomeTest Commercial lead and the Contract Management lead (post to be created)
Date taken13 August 2026
Review cadenceAfter every removal action, and at each reopening window

Position

Two levers, not one. Day-to-day performance is held in the call-off; the framework holds the register. Each call-off carries the KPIs for the service and the commercial recourse if they are missed; service credits are the worked example, and the commissioner exercises that recourse as the contracting party. Failure against the published assurance standard, or persistent failure against the call-off, escalates through a written protocol to removal from the accredited register, for a category or wholesale. Removal is decided on evidence against stated thresholds, signed by a named authority, notified to every commissioner calling off from that supplier, open to appeal, and fully audit-trailed. The cohort moves to another accredited supplier on the register, which is one reason the floor of about three suppliers per category matters.

Rationale

Every commissioner relies on the register without re-checking what the framework has already assured. That reliance only holds if admission is real and removal is real. A protocol that exists on paper but is never used sends the same signal to suppliers as no protocol at all: that the standard is advisory.

The call-off is where the money and the day-to-day service sit, so it is where routine performance belongs. The commissioner has the contract and the recourse. The framework’s job is the standard and the register: it acts when the failure is about the standard, or when it persists across call-offs.

Removal from a public register is a decision that will be challenged. It needs stated thresholds, proportional escalation, evidence, a named signatory, a right of appeal and a clean record before the first material breach, not after.

What this does not mean

Removal is not the only sanction any more. Earlier thinking, before the framework was settled, treated it as the sole lever because there was no call-off to terminate. There now is.

A supplier removed from one category is not automatically removed from all. The protocol distinguishes a category failure from a company failure.

Reopen triggers

  • A removal action is challenged successfully, in court, at the ICO or by ministerial intervention, and reveals a protocol gap.
  • A supplier insolvency exposes a gap in the wind-down and migration provisions.
  • A regulatory ruling sharpens the procedural standard for removal-from-public-register decisions.
  • The framework provider decision changes who signs, since the provider runs framework assurance under the construct.

Dependencies and open items

  • The performance regime in the framework agreement and the standard call-off: KPIs, recourse, escalation route.
  • Removal protocol written to production grade: thresholds, steps, evidence standards, decision authority, appeal, audit trail. A half-day design session for off-boarding and compelled removal is planned for October 2026 and has no owner yet.
  • Migration provisions so a cohort moves to another accredited supplier without a gap in service.
  • Contract management capability to run the regime after signature; the post is proposed, not yet created.
