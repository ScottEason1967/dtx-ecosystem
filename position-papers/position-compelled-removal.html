Compelled removal: the enforcement mechanism

Removal from the approved list is the only enforcement leverage NHSE holds in the interim model. The removal protocol must be defensible the first time it bites.

StatusActive
OwnerHomeTest Commercial lead and Senior sponsor
Date taken28 May 2026
Review cadenceAfter every removal action

Position

Removal from the approved supplier list is the primary enforcement leverage NHSE holds over supplier behaviour in the interim model. The Compelled route of Play 8 (Remove a supplier from the approved list) carries the protocol. Failure against the published assurance standard triggers escalation; persistent failure triggers compelled removal. The protocol must be written, defensible, and tested before the first material breach lands.

Rationale

The interim model has no framework call-off and therefore no termination clause for NHSE to invoke. NHSE is not a party to the bilateral contract between supplier and commissioner, so the contract is not NHSE's to terminate. The only sanction NHSE controls is removal of the supplier from the approved list, which prevents future commissioners contracting them and signals to existing commissioners that the supplier no longer carries NHSE assurance.

If the removal protocol is undocumented or weak, suppliers know there are no real consequences for sub-standard performance and the assurance standard becomes advisory. If the protocol is documented but never used, the same signal lands. Removal needs to be both real and rare.

This position raises the importance of offboarding compared with framework models where performance teeth sit in the call-off termination clause. Without a framework, removal is the only teeth that remain. The protocol therefore needs more weight, not less.

Reopen triggers

  • A removal action is challenged successfully (in court, at the ICO, by ministerial intervention) and reveals a protocol gap.
  • A regulatory ruling sharpens the procedural standard for removal-from-public-list decisions.
  • A framework call-off is later stood up, providing termination teeth that change the enforcement architecture (see Position · Framework status).
  • A supplier insolvency exposes a gap in the wind-down provisions.

Dependencies

  • Removal protocol authored. Play 8 currently describes the discipline. Production-grade writing needed: specific thresholds for escalation, named escalation steps, evidence standards, decision authority, audit trail requirements.
  • Appeal mechanism specified. Suppliers must know how to challenge a removal decision. Without an appeal route, the first contested removal becomes a judicial review case.
  • Audit trail discipline for removal-related decisions. Every escalation step recorded, every threshold breach documented, every sign-off named.
  • Communication protocol with affected commissioners. When a supplier is removed, commissioners with active contracts need to know within an agreed window and need to know what their options are.

Standard expectations of the protocol

The first contested removal sets the precedent. The protocol therefore needs to be robust enough to survive judicial review and ICO scrutiny on first contact. That means: clear failure thresholds against the published standard, proportional escalation before removal, evidence-led decision-making, named authority at senior-sponsor level signs the removal, supplier right of appeal, commissioner notification, full audit trail. Lean does not mean weak.
