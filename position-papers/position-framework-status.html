The commercial vehicle: the Open Framework

HomeTest’s enduring commercial vehicle is an NHSE-enabled Open Framework under the Procurement Act 2023: one framework, one end-to-end lot, suppliers assured once, commissioners calling off locally. Go-live calendar Q1 2028 in principle.

OwnerHomeTest Commercial lead with NHS Commercial
Date taken22 June 2026 (market form) and 13 August 2026 (shape)
Review cadenceAt each sourcing strategy checkpoint

Position

HomeTest’s enduring commercial vehicle is an Open Framework under the Procurement Act 2023, enabled by NHS England and operated by a framework provider. One framework agreement carries one end-to-end lot, request to result at home. Suppliers are admitted through two-tier assurance (the company once, each test at its gate on a published roadmap) and appear on an accredited register. Commissioners (local authorities, trusts, ICBs and pathology networks) call off from that register at or below a national maximum price and pay the supplier direct. The framework value is circa £250m over its life, with a term of up to five years (three plus one plus one). Go-live is calendar Q1 2028 in principle; a pull-forward to late 2027 is being tested and one candidate operator forecasts May 2028, so the date carries that range. The market form was chosen at the June 2026 SLT playback, where the Dynamic Market and the Digital Primary Care Framework option were set aside; the shape was settled at the August 2026 sourcing decisions.

Rationale

An open framework is the PA23 form that fits how this market has to grow. It reopens at windows (annual as the working assumption), admits new suppliers at capability gates, and can hold conditions of entry and a floor on supplier numbers. A dynamic market offered nothing genuinely dynamic for a market of this shape, would have taken around two years to stand up, and could not cap supplier numbers. The Digital Primary Care Framework could not bridge the near-term gap.

NHS England enabling the framework is what makes it worth joining. Every commissioner would otherwise run a bespoke procurement and every supplier would be assured once per buyer. The national vehicle carries the two things only NHSE brings: clinical validation and assurance of the whole request-to-result chain, and eligibility for orders through the NHS App. The framework provider brings the PA23-compliant machinery to run it.

The value and term are sized from the modelled test roadmap, ten tests at a ramp to twenty per cent utilisation by the end of the decade, and are under NHS Finance review. A case of this size runs the Commercial Executive Group, the DHSC Commercial Assurance Board and potentially the Joint Investment Committee, with Treasury and Cabinet Office in parallel; that approvals path, then tender from January 2027 and evaluation and award through 2027, is why go-live is Q1 2028 rather than earlier.

What this does not mean

It is not a pathology or logistics framework and must not become one. Component lots run by others, on a wider pathology framework for instance, are welcome alongside it; the home testing lot keeps its identity and its capability to deliver the whole service wherever it is hosted.

HomeTest does not procure on commissioners’ behalf and does not hold their budgets. It gives NHS organisations a build-or-buy choice and is agnostic between the two; an organisation that builds its own service is supported off-framework through a community of practice.

Nothing about the framework is final. The provider, the approvals, the value review and the September supplier and commissioner engagement can all move it, and the model says so wherever the framework appears.

Reopen triggers

  • The framework provider decision places home testing as a lot on another vehicle, in particular NHS Supply Chain’s replacement diagnostics framework, which goes to market in October 2026 with home testing earmarked. The alignment meeting is 23 September 2026.
  • Governance rejects or re-scopes the case at any approvals gate, or the NHS Finance review moves the value materially.
  • Supplier or commissioner engagement in September 2026 produces evidence that changes the shape (see The lotting choice).
  • The go-live date moves outside the stated range.

Dependencies and open items

  • Framework provider decision. External providers (LPP, SBS, NHS Supply Chain) scored against NHS running it itself; decision targeted September 2026, possibly October (see Who runs the framework).
  • Sourcing strategy submitted by 30 September 2026, with the lotting rationale, supplementary RFI responses (open to 30 September) and supplier and commissioner feedback in its evidence base.
  • ITT specification in drafting; performance criteria and evaluation criteria follow from it.
  • The published roadmap of test categories is not yet agreed; the framework cannot admit suppliers to categories it has not published.
