Framework status: operating in interim shape

No formal commercial framework in the FY26 to FY27 window. The commercial machinery is operating in the interim shape. Named reopen triggers govern when the framework conversation reopens.

StatusActive
OwnerHomeTest Commercial lead
Date taken28 May 2026
Review cadenceQuarterly portfolio review

Position

HomeTest is operating in the interim commercial shape. A formal commercial framework is not being stood up in this window. The commercial machinery operates through the Assurance wrapper (approved supplier list, published assurance standard, standard NHS call-off contract) with bilateral contracting between supplier and customer. NHSE convenes the system, assures supplier quality, and introduces approved suppliers to commissioners. NHSE is not a party to the resulting contract and takes no percentage of the bilateral transaction. The framework conversation reopens at named triggers. The Open Framework cannot stand up by April 2027; the targeted go-live is calendar Q1 2028 in principle, at a framework value of circa £250m over a maximum five-year term now under NHS Finance review. April 2027 is the full live HIV rollout target, protected by the interim route, with the private beta (September 2026) and public beta (late October to early November 2026) the 2026 proving phases before it.

Rationale

At interim portfolio scale of one to three live tests, take-rate revenues from a formal framework do not justify either the standup cost or the political surface of operating as a commercial counterparty. A multi-quarter standup window would also delay PSA scaling while market pressure from the men's health strategy builds. The cost of waiting is not absorbed by the value of the framework.

The matchmaker-with-assurance model preserves NHSE's authority over supplier quality (through the approved list and the published standard) without absorbing the commercial liability of being a contracting party. Bilateral contracting between supplier and commissioner uses existing NHS Standard Contract machinery. No new instruments to design, no novel legal architecture to defend.

Reopen triggers

The framework conversation reopens when any one of the following lands:

  • The portfolio approaches the scale where the take-rate would materially earn its keep. Working hypothesis around ten live tests on the approved list; the specific threshold is not yet locked.
  • Aggregate transacted volume across the portfolio crosses the scale where a percentage take becomes material. Specific volume threshold not yet locked.
  • Suppliers actively ask for the structure. The absence of a framework starts to constrain supplier behaviour or market entry.
  • NHSE Legal changes its position on the interim legal vehicle (see Position · Interim legal vehicle).
  • The FY27 planning round is reached and the next target standup window is set. Whichever position is taken at that point supersedes this paper.
  • A regulatory ruling, NAO finding or material patient-harm event challenges the matchmaker-without-framework architecture.

If the conversation reopens

PA23 is the recommended procurement regime per the Recommendation paper, supported by NHS Legal. The formal NHS Legal advice note will follow the recommendation rather than gate it. The within-PA23 market form has been chosen: an NHSE-administered Open Framework, confirmed at the 22 June 2026 SLT playback, with the PA23 Dynamic Market rejected. The direction is chosen and locked into the Recommendation paper v1.1; the formal governance approval route is still to be confirmed. The 20 July 2026 Programme Board noted the direction but could not approve it without senior commercial engagement, with Action HT052 to confirm the approval route. The framework administrator (host) has moved from open to a live three-way selection between an external provider and NHS running it itself: NHSE will appoint a public-sector host internally, with no open market for the operator itself. NHS SBS, NHS Supply Chain and London Procurement Partnership are in the run-off; SBS and LPP proposals are due 7 August 2026, a recommendation is targeted for end August 2026. The options matrix in Play 4 names the candidate routes and the reopen event would re-score them against the conditions at that point.

Dependencies

  • NHSE Legal formal advice note. NHS Legal is supportive of PA23. The formal advice note will follow the Recommendation paper rather than gate it; until landed, the position carries a residual legal-interpretation risk that NHSE Legal owns to resolve.
  • Approved supplier list register stood up. Names, scope, admission date, status. Started from zero and populated as suppliers come through.
  • Standard call-off template authored and signed off. Single template that wave entrants use; lighter operational annex tailoring than Beta partner MoUs.
  • Quarterly portfolio review carries the standing reopen check. The reopen triggers above are tested at each quarterly review against live-test count, transacted volume and supplier behaviour, so the interim shape is re-affirmed deliberately rather than persisting by default. Whichever position the FY27 planning round takes supersedes this paper.
