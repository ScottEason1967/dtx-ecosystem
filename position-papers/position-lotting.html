The lotting choice: one end-to-end lot

The framework buys the whole journey, request to result at home, as one lot. Capability lots were assessed and set aside. Held in principle, with conditions, because lots cannot be changed once the framework is live.

StatusHeld in principle · rationale recorded · conditions outstanding
OwnerFuture State team with NHS Commercial; Deputy Director as decision owner
Date taken13 August 2026; rationale recorded 2 September 2026
Review cadenceAt the September market engagement and the framework provider decision

Position

The Open Framework carries one end-to-end lot: home self-sampling as a service, request to result at home, with a community of practice alongside it rather than as a lot. Commissioners buy an outcome from one accountable provider per call-off; a prime, a consortium or an NHS-led arrangement delivers it, and suppliers holding part of the journey come in by partnering. The multi-lot alternative, capability lots for logistics, kits, laboratory analysis and digital service alongside an end-to-end lot, was assessed at a dedicated deep-dive and set aside; the comparison stays on record because no call in the sourcing strategy will draw more scrutiny. Under the Procurement Act lots are fixed on day one: reopenings add or remove suppliers within the lots that exist, so the choice has to be right first time.

Rationale

The clinical chain of custody. From request to result a sample and its data pass through several hands, and somebody assured has to own that chain, because there is no acceptable drop-off point. Bought in pieces, no single organisation owns the risk of a sample going missing, a result failing to flow or a step falling between two contracts. One lot puts that ownership in one place and lets the risk be delegated to the supplier that has taken it on.

The market already works this way. Home testing organisations hold the request-to-result chain as a block and assemble it from specialists: kits developed and approved through a kit specialist, logistics almost universally one tracked carrier, laboratory analysis in-house or contracted. Buying the chain end to end matches how it is sold.

Assurance comes from the pathology system. A supplier’s claim that it can safely join the chain up is evidenced through UKAS accreditation of the laboratory and MHRA conformity of the kit. The framework requires and checks that; it does not construct it. One lot keeps the requirement uniform; each capability lot would need its own assurance route.

Build or buy, and what a builder still buys. The framework is agnostic between NHS organisations building their own service and buying one. A builder still buys kits and logistics, and neither warrants a lot: kits vary too much to be price-listed and logistics is effectively a single provider. Both are signposted through the community of practice.

The counter-arguments on record

That the assurance and liability case for request-to-result is a contracting question, not a lotting one: a commissioner can be put on the hook through the call-off without that precluding other lots. Not answered in the room; a legal view may be needed.

That the supply base is thinner than the position assumes: for PSA there is no end-to-end supplier today, for HIV the market is effectively a duopoly with an existing framework already covering the tests, and the RFI showed how hard it is for smaller suppliers to assemble the whole journey. Recorded as “to be verified” in the rationale.

That a single lot with only two suppliers would draw accusations of favouring incumbents. Hence the condition that the lot shape is tested with suppliers before the strategy is finalised.

Reopen triggers

  • Supplier engagement in September 2026 (every supplier that registered interest, thirty-plus) shows a single lot cannot attract enough end-to-end bidders.
  • Commissioner evidence shows demand for component buying; engagement to date across sexual health, HIV and PSA reports none, and that is being written up.
  • The framework provider decision places the lot inside a wider pathology framework, where brand and capability must be protected.
  • A legal view that the chain-of-custody argument is answered by the call-off contract rather than by the lot.

Dependencies and open items

  • Supplier test of the framework principles including the lot shape at the September market update, ahead of the October forum; feedback on record for governance.
  • Commissioner evidence consolidated and structured sessions run; the strategy will not claim feedback that was not sought.
  • Contract construct: provider runs framework assurance, NHSE mandates baseline standards through a direct catalogue-type relationship; legal session to be set up.
  • Even-handedness between NHS and independent suppliers (ethical walls) to be designed; the community of practice’s offer and owner still open.
