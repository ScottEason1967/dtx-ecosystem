Interim legal vehicle: PA23 with standard call-off and approved supplier list

The legal architecture under which the interim commercial machinery operates. PA23 is the recommended procurement regime per the Recommendation paper.

StatusRecommended in the Recommendation paper; NHSE Legal supportive (formal advice note follows the recommendation rather than gates it)
OwnerHomeTest Commercial lead
Date taken21 June 2026
Review cadenceOn change to the within-PA23 market form or quarterly

Position

The interim commercial machinery operates under the Procurement Act 2023 (PA23) as the procurement regime, with the standard NHS call-off contract as the bilateral vehicle between supplier and customer, and an NHSE-curated approved supplier list as the qualification mechanism. NHSE issues the assurance standard and the call-off template. The bilateral contract is between the supplier and the commissioner. PA23 is the recommended procurement regime per the Recommendation paper, supported by NHS Legal. The formal NHS Legal advice note will follow the recommendation rather than gate it. Both PA23 and PSR remain defensible in principle. The within-PA23 market form is confirmed as an NHSE-administered Open Framework, chosen at the 22 June 2026 SLT playback, with the PA23 Dynamic Market rejected. The direction is chosen and locked into the Recommendation paper v1.1; the formal governance approval route is still to be confirmed (Programme Board 20 July 2026, Action HT052). Plan C is the live interim route: the NHS Online iron deficiency anaemia commission is live, the NHS SBS Sexual Health Framework (SBS10255) carries HIV with direct award where regulations allow, and Alpha and Beta MOUs continue to cover live deployments.

Rationale

PA23 provides the procurement vehicle with the flexibility the operating model needs. It carries supplier qualification against a published standard (which is what the assurance wrapper does) and accommodates the periodic admission with a supplier cap that the chosen NHSE-administered Open Framework uses. The within-PA23 market form was settled at the 22 June 2026 SLT playback.

The approved-list discipline replaces the bid mechanics of a framework with continuous qualification against the published standard. Suppliers apply, NHSE assesses against the standard, suppliers are admitted or refused. The list is maintained as a live register, not snapshotted at procurement windows.

The standard NHS call-off template prevents twenty different bespoke contracts emerging across twenty commissioners. Existing NHS Standard Contract machinery and call-off variants do the contracting work; the playbook's job is to specify which schedules are pre-filled from the proven pathway and which are commissioner-specific.

Design principles the architecture enacts

Four design principles run through the choice of PA23 plus standard call-off plus approved list. The architecture is shaped to deliver each one rather than treating them as constraints to work around.

  • Interoperability as the admission test. The published assurance standard sets the bar suppliers admit against, with interoperability requirements baked into it. Suppliers that do not meet the standard cannot sell into the platform. The gate sits in front of the marketplace, not after it, which prevents the broken-market pattern where every commissioner ends up with a different supplier locking them in on different terms.
  • Anti-lock-in carried in the schedules. The standard call-off template hard-codes data portability, open-standards expectations and costed exit provisions into the schedules every commissioner uses. The bilateral contract sits between supplier and commissioner; the protections that make exit affordable for the commissioner are NHSE's contribution to the template.
  • The approved list as the public option. Commissioners do not have to procure from cold. The list gives every commissioner a working starting set of NHSE-assured suppliers to contract from. Where a commissioner would otherwise be trapped with whichever supplier they happened to buy from first, the list creates a way out by giving them alternatives that meet the same standard.
  • One marketplace, not twenty. The within-PA23 market form, the NHSE-administered Open Framework, is the consolidation move. Twenty bespoke commissioner-by-commissioner procurements collapse into one register against one published standard. Suppliers prepare and apply once; commissioners contract through one mechanism.

Reopen triggers

  • NHSE Legal formal advice note arrives with a position different from what the Recommendation paper anticipates.
  • A regulatory ruling, ICO finding or NAO study challenges the PA23-plus-approved-list architecture.
  • Any of the framework-status reopen triggers fire (see Position · Framework status).
  • The approved-list register encounters a removal action that exposes a protocol gap requiring a different legal footing.

Dependencies

  • Written NHSE Legal view on PSR applicability. The load-bearing dependency. Verbal confirmation is not sufficient; the architecture rests on a written position.
  • Standard call-off template authored. Either using NHS Standard Contract framing or a defined call-off variant. Schedules pre-filled from the proven pathway template; commissioner-specific bits left to local edit.
  • Approved supplier list register stood up. Format, governance, admission and removal protocols specified.
  • Published assurance standard. The substantive criteria suppliers apply against. Consolidated from existing HIV Alpha and Beta IG envelope, clinical safety baseline and lab assurance material.

Reversibility

If NHSE Legal concludes PSR is not the right vehicle, the model needs reconfiguration but the constituent artefacts survive. The approved list, the assurance standard and the standard call-off template all transfer into whatever alternative architecture is required. The lost work is the legal-architecture layer, not the operational substance.
