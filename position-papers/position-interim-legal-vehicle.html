The interim route: how commissioners buy until the framework lands

Until the Open Framework is live, commissioners buy through existing frameworks and memoranda of understanding. It is guidance to commissioners, not a new vehicle, and it runs to around January 2028.

StatusLive · retires into the Open Framework
OwnerHomeTest Commercial lead with the Future State team
Date taken22 June 2026, instruments live from July 2026
Review cadenceAs each proof point exits Beta, and at Open Framework go-live

Position

Until the Open Framework is live, commissioners buy home testing through existing frameworks and memoranda of understanding, on guidance from the programme. The instruments are the NHS SBS Sexual Health Framework (SBS10255) for HIV, with direct award where the regulations allow; the London Procurement Partnership public health services framework (lot 1 sexual health and HIV at home, lot 5 NHS health checks and cancer screening at home); the NHS Online iron deficiency anaemia pathway, the first live instance of the route, commissioned in July 2026 and now being wound down; and NHS England to local authority memoranda of understanding, which serve Alpha and Beta only. The current Beta supply runs on the ACE arrangement; local authorities hold their own sexual health contracts for test delivery. The interim route is commissioner-led: NHSE assures suppliers and introduces them, and is not the commercial counterparty for the tests. It protects the April 2027 HIV rollout and the PSA active surveillance pilot (go-live indicatively February 2027) while the Open Framework is built, and retires into the framework as it lands.

Rationale

The Open Framework cannot stand up before calendar Q1 2028, and the programme has commitments before then: HIV Private Beta from late September 2026 with Public Beta from December 2026 to March 2027, the full HIV rollout target in April 2027, and the acute PSA pilot. Existing frameworks carry those lawfully now, with no novel legal architecture to design or defend. Both PA23 and PSR remain defensible routes; the enduring regime is PA23.

Memoranda of understanding were right for Alpha and Beta because no formal vehicle existed. They are not a BAU instrument: twenty bespoke MOUs across twenty commissioners would proliferate variance, undermine the standard call-off and weaken the audit trail. Wave entrants sign the standard call-off with the operational annex carrying the variation.

The proof-point routes were stood up to prove the model at small scale, not to carry portfolio scale. They are not the commercial model and are not presented as such; each transitions to the framework shape as it matures.

What this does not mean

It is not a second framework and not a fallback that competes with the Open Framework. It is the set of existing routes a commissioner can use today, with the programme’s guidance on which fits which cohort.

The route that carries HIV Public Beta at public volumes is not yet settled and is an open item; the interim instruments cover live deployments today.

Reopen triggers

  • Open Framework go-live: commissioners migrate on an agreed switchover schedule and the interim route winds down.
  • NHSE Legal’s written view on the interim shape changes, or an instrument is challenged.
  • An interim instrument expires, or cannot carry the volumes Public Beta needs.
  • NHS Supply Chain’s diagnostics framework offers an earlier lawful route for home testing.

Dependencies and open items

  • NHSE Legal written sign-off on the interim shape, so the architecture rests on more than a verbal position.
  • Standard call-off template authored and published; without it MOUs become the default by absence of alternative.
  • Commissioner guidance (playbook v1) on which route fits which cohort, and the switchover plan to the Open Framework.
  • Public Beta commercial route settled before public volumes arrive.
