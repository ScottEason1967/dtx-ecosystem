Who runs the framework, and NHS England’s role

NHS England sets the standard, assures the chain, holds the NHS App and takes a defined share of the levy back. A framework provider runs the framework. NHSE holds no budget and touches no payment.

StatusAgreed in principle · provider selection live
OwnerNHS Commercial with the Future State team
Date taken13 August 2026
Review cadenceAt the framework provider decision, then quarterly

Position

NHS England is the standards-setter, gate-opener and levy-taker for home testing, not the buyer and not the payment intermediary. NHSE owns the platform and the assurance standard, decides which test categories open and when through the published roadmap, holds the NHS App route that gives suppliers orders without building their own permission, and receives a defined share of the framework levy to reinvest. A framework provider operates the framework agreement, the call-off mechanics and the supplier levy. That provider is under live selection, scored three ways: external providers (LPP and SBS proposals are in, NHS Supply Chain is engaged) against NHS running it itself. The decision is targeted for September 2026 and may run to October. Commissioners hold the budget and pay suppliers direct; NHSE is not a party to those contracts.

Rationale

The separation puts each party where its authority sits. NHSE can set and hold a national standard and open the NHS App; it has no wish to hold commissioners’ budgets or sit in the payment flow, which would make it the counterparty being sued when a supplier underperforms. A framework provider already runs PA23-compliant machinery at scale and levies suppliers in a form the market knows.

The three-way scoring is deliberate. An external provider brings machinery and speed; NHS running it itself keeps control and the return in-house. NHS Supply Chain adds a live external factor: its replacement Diagnostics, Laboratory Testing and Point of Care framework, with home testing earmarked, goes to market in October 2026, so the choice is also between a standalone home testing framework and home testing as one lot on their vehicle. The alignment meeting is 23 September 2026.

Landing HomeTest as a non-clinical service under PA23 keeps the external providers in play; the interim iron deficiency anaemia route is kept separate from this decision.

What this does not mean

NHSE is not absent. It curates the register, sets and revises the assurance standard, runs the clinician panels and the joint IG forum, leads the engagement front end and carries the political accountability for the programme. Standing outside the payment flow is a structural choice about liability, not a withdrawal from convening authority.

The provider does not own the standard. Whoever operates the framework, the baseline standards are mandated by NHSE through a direct relationship, on a GP IT-like construct where the provider runs framework assurance and NHSE holds the catalogue-type relationship with suppliers. The legal session to confirm that construct is to be set up.

Reopen triggers

  • The framework provider decision lands (September or October 2026), including a decision to place home testing on NHS Supply Chain’s vehicle.
  • A patient-harm or supplier-failure event implicates NHSE as a party regardless of the formal model.
  • Treasury or Strategic Finance direction requires NHSE to operate as a revenue-collecting counterparty.
  • The return leg of the levy cannot be made to work lawfully (see How the money works).

Dependencies and open items

  • Provider decision and the realistic start dates each candidate can offer against the £250m framework; one candidate forecasts May 2028.
  • Brand and capability protection if the lot sits inside another provider’s framework.
  • Contract construct agreed with legal input: framework agreement, call-off agreement, capability accreditation folded in as schedules.
  • Named counterparts for integration, incident routes and uptime on the foundation services the framework consumes (NHS App, NHS Login, NHS Notify).
