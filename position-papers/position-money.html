How the money works

Commissioners pay suppliers direct at a cost per activity, below a national ceiling. Suppliers pay the framework provider a levy on throughput. A defined share comes back to NHS England and is reinvested. The return leg is the critical assumption.

StatusAgreed in principle · return leg is an assumption until settled
OwnerNHS Commercial with NHS Finance; Future State team
Date taken13 August 2026
Review cadenceOn the return-leg resolution (due end September 2026), then at each sourcing strategy checkpoint

Position

No money flows through NHS England. It moves straight between commissioner and supplier, with a share of throughput coming back to pay for the framework. The commissioner pays the supplier direct, per activity, at or below a national maximum price set for each test. Every stage of the cycle is priced (delivery, sample, analysis, result), so a cycle that completes seventy per cent of its stages is paid seventy per cent; completion is measured but not paid on, because a supplier does not control every reason a kit fails to come back. The supplier pays the framework provider a levy of roughly one to two per cent of its throughput through the framework, in the form other frameworks use. A defined share of that levy returns to NHS England as a platform fee on throughput, taken out of the provider levy rather than invoiced separately, and is reinvested in the clinician panels, the validation service, NHS App integration and pathway change. The model only earns when the market sells. Volume tiers are still open.

Rationale

Why suppliers pay: three things come with NHS England that no other route offers. Clinical validation and assurance of the whole chain, which every commissioner can then rely on without re-checking; orders through the national app without building a route to permission of their own; and investment in the pathway change that makes home sampling the default.

Why a national ceiling rather than a national price: the ceiling is a maximum. The commissioner runs its own call-off from the register, and competition and volume push the realised price down. The more accredited suppliers a category holds, the further below the ceiling it falls, which is why the floor of about three suppliers per category matters commercially as well as for resilience.

Why cost per activity rather than per completed test: it pays for what happened and keeps the invoice simple. If it takes a team to work out an invoice, the design is wrong. Performance is handled through the KPIs in the call-off, with commercial recourse in the contract; service credits are the worked example.

The critical assumption

The return leg is not decided. What the charge is called, the mechanism that brings the money back to NHSE, its VAT treatment and the competition risk are all open, owned by NHS Commercial and NHS Finance and due for resolution by the end of September 2026. Until they are settled the return line stays an assumption and the model says so wherever it appears.

The return is a platform fee on throughput and a contribution to development. It may not cover everything NHSE spends, and that is accepted. It is never described as anything else.

Pricing fairness is open. The intent is prices that fall as volume rises, with early buyers never paying more than later, bigger ones. That sits in tension with a market with no cap on supplier numbers, and whether it forces a bidder cap is undecided; both positions stay in the model.

Reopen triggers

  • The return-leg resolution (nomenclature, mechanism, VAT, competition) lands, or fails to.
  • The framework provider decision changes the levy mechanics or its level.
  • NHS Finance’s review of the volumes and value (week commencing 8 September 2026) moves the numbers.
  • The volume tier and bidder cap question is decided.

Dependencies and open items

  • Cost-per-activity definition per stage of the cycle, and the national ceiling design and price book.
  • Whether national ceilings can be renegotiated on reopening, and whether existing suppliers reprice.
  • NHSE run-cost and break-even against the levy share, so the reinvestment promise is sized.
  • The performance regime in the framework agreement and call-off: KPIs, recourse, escalation (see Supplier performance and removal).
