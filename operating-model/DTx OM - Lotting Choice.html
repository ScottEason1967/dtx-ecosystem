The commercial model · the decision that shapes it

The Lotting Choice.

One end-to-end lot is the working position, reached in principle and now being re-validated before the sourcing strategy is finalised, because no call in the strategy will draw more scrutiny. This page holds that comparison.

Working draft, off-live. Nothing here gets treated as settled until the team and the market have tested it.

The two options

Either could work. Before one is chosen, the strategy has to show that both options were fully assessed and that the rationale rests on evidence.

Option A · the working position

One end-to-end lot

The whole journey, bought as one package.
  • Commissioners buy an outcome. One accountable provider per call-off.
  • A prime, a consortium or an NHS-led arrangement delivers it.
  • Suppliers holding part of the journey come in by partnering: do what you can, team for the rest.
  • One assurance construct: the organisation accredited once, then per test at the gates.
The risks. Can be seen as favouring suppliers that already operate end-to-end. Niche suppliers can only enter by finding partners, so support for forming partnerships has to be part of the model.
Option B · the alternative

Capability lots

The framework as a marketplace of parts.
  • Lots drawn by organisational capability: logistics, kits, lab analysis, digital service.
  • Suppliers join the lots they can serve, and complete services are assembled from the parts.
  • A visible way in for niche players and for pathology networks that cannot do end-to-end alone.
  • The available parts are visible, so suppliers can form their own partnerships.
The risks. Stage-two assurance breaks into per-capability packs, each one built and run separately. Accountability for the end-to-end result still has to sit somewhere. And the structure can be seen as favouring NHS delivery instead.
Same supplier, two journeys

The straw-man onboarding journey under each option. Neither option removes the friction; it moves. Under Option A it sits before entry, under Option B after entry.

Under one end-to-end lot

1

See the test roadmap and the reopening windows. The timeline is published.

2

Assemble the offer: bid alone, form or join a consortium, or come in NHS-led.

3

Stage one: organisation accreditation, once, onto the framework.

4

Stage two: per-test accreditation as each roadmap gate opens.

5

Supply into call-offs. Paid per activity. Levy applies.

The friction: step 2. Without partners, a capable niche supplier cannot enter. Early roadmap visibility and deliberate introductions address this.

Under capability lots

1

See the roadmap. Same timeline, same gates.

2

Apply into the capability lot you serve.

3

Stage one accreditation for the lot.

4

Stage two in a capability-specific form: a logistics pack is not a lab pack.

5

Get assembled into a chain. Open: who assembles it, and who answers for the whole when a part fails.

6

Supply the component. Accountability for the end-to-end result sits above the parts.

The friction: steps 4 and 5. Every capability needs its own assurance pack, and the integrator question has no owner yet.
Four lenses

The follow-up session runs both options through each lens. If one option wins on a single lens and loses on the other three, the decision is clear.

Assurance and administration

A: one construct, two stages, one pack to maintain.

B: a stage-two pack per capability, more to build and more to administer. This is the strongest internal concern about lots.

Market shape

The bias risk runs in both directions. A can read as built for end-to-end incumbents. B can read as built for NHS pathology delivery. Either way, the strategy has to demonstrate balance and set out how suppliers not directly served can still participate.

What commissioners buy

A: an outcome, with one accountable provider per call-off.

B: components they assemble, with the integration burden somewhere between buyer and framework.

Commissioner preference is untested. It is being asked directly, not assumed.

Patients and the service

However the market is structured, the patient experiences one journey. A puts accountability for that journey in one place. B has to engineer it. The service has to work under either lotting outcome.

What settles it

Evidence required before the position hardens

  • The interoperability constraint, documented. If the case for one lot rests on kit and laboratory method incompatibility, that needs to be documented evidence, not an assertion made in a meeting.
  • The commissioner answer. Do buyers want a results service or their own supply chain? Asked directly, before the strategy is fixed.
  • The supplier-market read. Both shapes go to the market at the next engagement round, and the feedback goes on record for governance.
  • Partnering support. If one lot is confirmed, partnering support becomes a requirement of the simpler structure: roadmap visibility, reopening windows and deliberate introductions.
The test the rationale has to pass. A single lot chosen because it suits incumbent end-to-end suppliers will not survive scrutiny. A lot structure chosen for the appearance of openness, but which cannot be assured or administered, will not work in operation. The chosen option has to be supported by evidence and simple to operate.
Working position: one end-to-end lot · held open until the rationale is evidenced · decided with the sourcing strategy
