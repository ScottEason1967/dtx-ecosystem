How commissioners buy.

The buyers in the model
The mechanics

Buying works through six positions. Five are agreed in principle; volume tiers are still open.

Call-offs below a national maximum price

Each test carries a national maximum price set by NHSE. The commissioner runs its own call-off from the register, and from there competition and volume push the price down. NHSE shares forecasts; it does not guarantee volumes.

Entry terms, price fundamentals and the performance regime sit in the framework agreement; the order and the local terms sit in the call-off. Accreditation is in the schedules.

Under one end-to-end lot, the call-off buys the whole journey from one accredited provider, who answers for the end-to-end result.

Under capability lots, the call-off assembles parts. open Who integrates them, and who answers for the end-to-end result, is not designed.

Paid per activity, not per completed test

Every stage of the cycle is priced: delivery, sample, analysis, result. A cycle that completes 70 percent of its stages is paid 70 percent.

The programme measures completion but does not pay on it, because a supplier does not control every reason a kit fails to come back. The contract KPIs carry the remedies. No service credits.

The settlement lane on How →

Money flows directly

Invoices run from supplier to commissioner at the rates and frequency the call-off sets, and the commissioner pays from its own budget. NHSE holds no budget and touches no payment.

The money loop →

Choice on the register

Every accredited supplier in every open category shows on the register. Commissioners compare price and whatever sits above the minimum standard: device, speed, service. The more suppliers a category holds, the further prices fall below the ceiling.

Under one lot, comparison is provider against provider for the whole journey.

Under capability lots it runs per component, and someone has to assemble the chain. open

How suppliers get on →

Volume tiers: open

The intent is pricing that falls as volume rises, with fairness built in so early buyers never end up paying more than later, bigger ones. That is in tension with a market that has no cap on supplier numbers.

Both options stay in the model. No decision yet.

An NHS organisation can serve itself

Rather than buy from the framework, a commissioner or a pathology network can stand up its own home sampling. NHSE supports that through a community of practice and a concierge service, off the framework.

What changes for buyers under capability lots

Buying an outcome becomes assembling one: delivery, kit, lab analysis and digital service come from separate lots, there are more contracts per pathway, and the integration burden falls somewhere between buyer and framework. open The integrator role has no owner yet, and no commissioner has said they want it. The comparison is on the Lotting Choice page.

What buyers have said so far

Commissioner engagement to date, across sexual health, HIV and prostate cancer, reports no buyer wanting to assemble components; those who buy today buy a full pathway and expect to keep doing so. That evidence is being consolidated, and structured sessions with buyers are still to run; what comes back will change this page.

The questions the buyers get asked

  • What does good look like for you? In your own words, before we show you ours.
  • An outcome or a supply chain? A results service with one accountable provider, or parts you assemble and integrate yourself?
  • What is wrong with how you buy this today? The framework has to improve on the current route, which means understanding where the current route fails.
  • Would tiered pricing change your decision? Do volume tiers matter to you, or is certainty of price worth more than a discount?
  • Who owns integration? Are you content for the provider to integrate into your systems and pathways, or does that stay with you?
  • What would let you trust the register? What evidence or assurance would you need before you stop re-checking what the framework has already assured?
  • When would you first buy? What has to be true, and by when, for you to use this route instead of your current one?
