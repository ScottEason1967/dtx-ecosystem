What Ruth is trying to do
Close a monitoring gap that nobody currently owns
Men referred to Urology with a raised PSA and no cancer found go back to their GP with an instruction to keep an eye on it. Some are monitored well. Some drift. The cohort has no single budget holder, no agreed cadence and no one measuring whether the monitoring happens. Ruth's job is to make that cohort someone's responsibility, with a supplier behind it that has already been assured.
What the model already gives her
A configuration that exists, and five questions it cannot answer for her
PSA active surveillance is the board-selected acute use case, scored Priority 2 (35/45) under the Prioritisation Framework. The kit, the labs, the NHS App journey and the supplier assurance are the same ones Mark's cohort uses. What the model deliberately holds open is who Ruth is: her institution, her budget source, the clinical owner, the protocol cadence and the contract shape. Those come from the cohort discovery, not from the operating model.

What the model is doing underneath
The Prioritisation Framework has already placed PSA active surveillance as the live clinician-initiated configuration, and the monitoring-gap cohort is scoped as a sub-cohort inside it rather than scored on its own. That matters for Ruth: she is not making the case for a new test category. She is making the case for a defined group of men to be attached to a configuration the platform already runs. The Status page carries the cohort as TBC, which is the model's way of saying the dependency is known and the answer is still being worked.
The old way
The raised-PSA, no-diagnosis patient is discharged back to primary care with advice. Whether he is recalled depends on the practice, the patient and luck. Secondary care does not see him again unless he re-presents. No one holds the list, so no one can say how many men are in it, how many were monitored on time and how many were lost.
👥
What the care team sees
Urology sees the cohort as men it has already assessed and cannot keep on its books. Primary care sees them as follow-ups it was never resourced to run. Both are right, which is exactly why the budget question comes first.

What the model is doing underneath
The framework has done the national work in advance: Tier 1 company assurance through the Master SCAL, Tier 2 conformance for the PSA category, UKAS-accredited labs, MHRA-conformant kits, clinical safety cases under DCB0129 and DCB0160. Sarah leaned on exactly this for HIV; Catherine for the established active surveillance cohort. Ruth inherits it. Whichever institution she turns out to sit in, the accredited register is the same and the AQP floor of around three suppliers per test holds, in principle, so she is buying from a live market rather than a single incumbent.
The old way
A local procurement for a monitoring service, or more likely no procurement at all: a spreadsheet, a recall letter template and a nurse who knows the list. Assurance, if it happened, was repeated Trust by Trust with the same questions asked of the same suppliers.
👥
What the care team sees
The clinical team's question changes from 'is this kit safe and is this lab any good' to 'does this protocol fit our patients'. The first was answered nationally. The second is theirs.

What the model is doing underneath
Here the model stops and waits, deliberately. The surveillance protocol for men with a raised PSA and no diagnosis is being developed by the team scoping the cohort. The candidate cadences are on the table: annual, biannual, risk-stratified or symptom-led. The platform can run any of them, because the initiation pattern is clinician-initiated with a scheduled recall, the same pattern Mark's cohort uses. What the model refuses to do is pick the cadence for the clinicians. Until the protocol is signed off, Ruth has nothing to commission against, and the model says so rather than papering over it.
The old way
Cadence was whatever the discharge letter said, interpreted by whichever GP read it. No protocol meant no way to audit compliance, and no way to tell a commissioner what good looked like.
👥
What the care team sees
Whoever ends up clinically owning the cohort, Urology, primary care, a shared monitoring service or a new owner, gets the protocol as a configuration on the platform: eligibility rule, recall schedule, result thresholds and the escalation route back into the clinic. The care team defines it once and the platform enforces it every time.

What the model is doing underneath
The framework was built so that the contract shape follows the budget owner rather than the other way round. If the cohort lands in an ICB pathway budget, Ruth's route is a call-off from the accredited register, the way Sarah and Catherine buy. If it lands in an acute tariff envelope, the call-off is made by the Trust with Ruth's ICB as the commissioning counterparty. If it needs a joint arrangement or earmarked national funding, the framework's terms still apply and only the paying party changes. In every version the price is a cost per activity for each completed test, against a national ceiling that local competition can beat, with a levy on throughput that funds the platform and returns a share to NHS England. All of that is in principle until the cohort discovery names the owner.
The old way
A bespoke local contract, or a grant, or a service that ran on goodwill and then stopped when the goodwill did. Terms negotiated from scratch, with no national ceiling and no recourse the commissioner could actually use.
👥
What the care team sees
Nothing changes clinically at the point of contract. The care team's protocol is already on the platform; the buy simply switches it on for their patients and tells the supplier who is paying.

What the model is doing underneath
Once live, the cohort behaves like any clinician-initiated configuration on HomeTest. Eligible men are enrolled from the clinic, the platform issues the kit on the protocol's schedule, the result returns through the accredited lab to the clinician, and the NHS App carries the patient's view. Missed recalls surface as exceptions rather than silence. Results above the threshold route straight back to Urology through the escalation rule the care team set in Stage 03. Ruth gets the operating data commissioners never had for this group: enrolment, on-time completion, loss to follow-up and escalations.
The old way
Recall depended on the practice system flagging it and someone acting on the flag. A man who moved practice, changed his number or simply did not respond disappeared from a list that was never really a list.
👥
What the care team sees
Urology sees only the men who need to come back. Primary care sees a cohort being monitored without the practice carrying the admin. Both can see the same record because the NHS holds it, not the supplier.

What the model is doing underneath
The monitoring-gap case rests on early detection: the men in this cohort who go on to develop diagnosable prostate cancer, found on schedule rather than on re-presentation. The outcome measure is still to be defined alongside the protocol, and the model says so. What the platform gives Ruth from day one is the denominator and the numerator she never had: how many men are monitored, how many on time, how many escalated and what was found. That is the evidence base for whichever institution funds the next year, and for the wider question of whether this cohort should be commissioned everywhere or only where a gap is proven.
The old way
No baseline, so no case. Commissioners were asked to fund monitoring for a group whose size and outcomes nobody could state.
👥
What the care team sees
Clinicians get an audit trail they can stand behind at the MDT and at inspection. The protocol, the compliance against it and the outcomes sit in one place.
Ruth's win, when it lands

A cohort that belongs to someone, monitored on a protocol, bought on terms already settled.

Ruth's journey is the honest one in this cast. Five of its specifics are held open on purpose: her institution, her budget source, the clinical owner, the protocol cadence and the contract shape. What the model shows is that none of those blockers sits in the platform or the framework. The assurance is done, the configuration exists, the buy route follows whichever owner emerges, and the operating data starts on day one. When the cohort discovery lands, Ruth becomes a fully populated persona on Sarah's pattern and this journey firms up with her.

The money in this story

Ruth buys nothing until the cohort has an owner, and the model is explicit about that. When she does buy, the mechanics are the ones already built for Sarah and Catherine: a call-off from the accredited register, a cost per activity for each completed test against a national ceiling, local competition able to beat that ceiling, and a levy on throughput that funds the platform with a share returned to NHS England.

What her cohort adds is the funding question itself. A group that sits between primary and secondary care needs a paying party named before any of that machinery can turn, which is why the budget owner sits at Stage 01 of this story and not at the end.
