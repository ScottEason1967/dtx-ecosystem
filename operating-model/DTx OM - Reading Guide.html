How to read this operating model.

An interactive Future State Blueprint for the Self Health Services portfolio: HomeTest, HealthStore and HealthSync. Built so every key player can see themselves in the future world and work with us on how to move there, across every discipline of BAU.

About the scope

From HomeTest discovery into the wider portfolio.

The brief that opened this work was a commercial Target Operating Model for HomeTest. As that work moved, it became clear the commercial question (what vehicle delivers this, and how) couldn't be answered properly without showing the operating reality the vehicle has to support. The discovery forced the architecture, the cast, the lifecycle and the open questions into the open. Those learnings now carry across the wider Self Health Services portfolio.

The HomeTest commercial work is still here. It is the proving ground and it informs every layer. But it sits inside a broader portfolio frame, alongside HealthStore and HealthSync. That broader frame is a Future State Blueprint for the portfolio: the bridge between where each product is today and the future operating model the sponsor's direction and the wider portfolio thinking already point at.

First, the framing

This isn't a frozen operating model. It's an interactive Future State Blueprint.

A target operating model, in the traditional sense, is a snapshot, what ‘good’ looks like at a point in time. Useful, but static. This is more than that. It carries the snapshot inside it, alongside the present-day reality across all three products and the moves between them. And it stays live as the work moves.

The central insight this is built around
The service only works if the ecosystem works.
Not technology. Not procurement. Not policy. Not the platform. The ecosystem. That is why the architecture has to hold patients, clinicians, commissioners and suppliers in the same frame across HomeTest, HealthStore and HealthSync, and why adoption is the first-order design requirement, not a downstream consideration. Every layer of this blueprint is built around the question: how do we build a portfolio all of these players choose to use?
01 · The traditional view
A target operating model is a frozen snapshot.
It describes how a service ought to run, at one moment in time. Useful for sign-off. Useful for a board. But it doesn't hold the path from where you are now to where you need to be. And once it's signed, it ages.

A Future State Blueprint for the portfolio.
It shows where each of the three products is today, where the portfolio needs to get to, and the moves between. The static TOM is one of the things it produces, not the thing itself. The same is true for the playbooks across disciplines (commercial, IG and beyond), the position papers and the Hearts and Minds decks. The model stays live; the artefacts get reissued as the work moves.
03 · Why this matters
The 10 Year Plan set a destination. It didn't set out the HOW.
This is the HOW for HomeTest, HealthStore and HealthSync together. The architecture, the cast, the lifecycle, the open questions, the human stories. Built so every key player can see themselves in the future and contribute to the path between now and then.

The service itself

Three products, three service shapes.

Each product in the portfolio runs on a different shape on the ground. The architecture, cast and lifecycle that wrap them are common; the service flow inside each is product-specific. Every page of this blueprint expands one or more of these shapes.

HomeTest
Three steps.
Diagnostic episode
1
Referral. Demand finds the platform. Client-neutral, receives orders from clinicians, citizens or screening services.
2
Fulfilment. The kit goes out, the sample comes back. Ordering, dispatch, sample collection, lab processing.
3
Result. The result reaches the right place. Back to the patient, the GP record, the clinician. Reactive support sits underneath.
The HomeTest ‘Black Box’ shape. HIV today, PSA next, the wider test catalogue from there.
HealthStore
Four steps.
Therapeutic intervention
1
Catalogue listing. A digital therapeutic is admitted to the platform via the supplier onboarding process.
2
Commissioner adoption. Commissioners pick from the listed therapeutics and route them into their local pathway design.
3
Provider onboarding. Local providers wrap the therapeutic into prescribing flows, NHS App entry and clinical wrap.
4
Renewal and evaluation. Performance data feeds re-evaluation; catalogue listing renews, refreshes or expires.
COPD alpha first. Cardiac rehab as UC2. MSK third. Each new use case follows the same four-step shape.
HealthSync
Shape forming.
Continuous monitoring
1
Device pairing. A wearable or consumer health device is paired to the NHS App via Apple HealthKit or Google Health Connect.
2
Continuous stream. Readings flow back continuously rather than as a discrete test. The shape is monitoring, not episode.
3
Clinical interpretation. Data lands where the clinical conversation happens, not as a one-off result.
BP is the lead configuration. The full operating shape is forming as the Business Case lands and HealthSync stands up its delivery team.

Three different shapes describe what each product does. The five questions below describe how the model is organised to support them, common architecture across all three.

What to expect

Three honest notes before you click in.

The model is information-rich on purpose. You are not meant to read it linearly. Click around. Open something. Follow the cross-links. Come back to this page if you get lost.

Status and Teamwork are honest about what is not yet known. The list of open decisions is long because the work is mid-flight. The list is not the failure. It is the structure that makes the open questions answerable.

Hearts and Minds is the human layer. Five journeys in total. If you want a five-minute version of the whole portfolio, open Errol's Journey, the cross-product story that walks HealthSync, HomeTest and HealthStore in one arc. If you want the access story, open Sean's.
