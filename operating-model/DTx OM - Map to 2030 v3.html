The Map to 2030

FY 27/28
Open Framework goes live
NHSE-enabled Open Framework under PA23, go-live in calendar Q1 2028 in principle at the circa £250m framework value. One end-to-end lot; suppliers admitted through two-tier assurance (the company once, each test at its gate); commissioners hold the budget, call off at or below a national ceiling and pay suppliers direct. NHSE owns the platform and the assurance standards. The interim route retires into the framework as it lands.

Framework operating
Open Framework running. Framework provider bedded in (an external provider or NHS itself, decided autumn 2026). Provider levy on supplier throughput of roughly one to two per cent, with a defined share back to NHSE. Annual reopening admits new suppliers at capability gates; no new lots.

From pilots to ramping waves

The chips and volume bands above describe a deliberate pilot-to-scale arc, not a sequence of disconnected pilots. Private Beta proves the architecture works at low volume. A sustained safety record, qualifying citizen experience and standard-conforming supplier behaviour are the design tests, not the volume itself. Public Beta opens the architecture to roughly a thousand self-initiating citizens and tests what changes when the safety net of intense NHSE supervision thins out: the published standard, the assurance discipline and the removal protocol have to hold the line that NHSE direct conversation no longer can.

Wave 1 onboards only when the Public Beta findings demonstrate stable safety, scalable supplier conformance, a working citizen-facing surface and commissioner adoption of the approved-list discipline. The bridge between Public Beta closing and Wave 1 starting is the most consequential transition in the four-year arc. What carries across is the operating shape itself; the supplier base, test catalogue and commissioner cast extend with each wave but the architecture they run on stays constant.
