The commercial model · the money

The money loop.

No money flows through NHSE. It moves straight between commissioner and supplier, with a share of throughput coming back to pay for the framework. The model only earns when the market sells.

Working draft, off-live. Agreed in principle. The return leg is the critical open item; the note below sets out why.

The loop

Four steps, then it starts again.

1

The commissioner pays the supplier

Direct, per activity, at or below the national maximum price. NHSE holds no budget and touches no payments.

Under one lot: one provider per call-off, one invoice chain.

Under capability lots: who gets paid, each component supplier or a lead, is not designed. open

→
2

The supplier pays the levy

The framework provider takes 1 to 2 percent of the supplier's throughput. Other frameworks charge the same way, so suppliers are familiar with it. It applies to sales only.

Under one lot: one levy point per call-off holder.

Under capability lots: levy collection multiplies across component suppliers.

→
3

A share returns to NHSE

A platform fee: a percentage of throughput, taken out of the provider levy. Not a separate invoice to suppliers.

→
4

NHSE reinvests it

The clinician panels, the validation service, the app integration and pathway change get paid from it. It may not cover everything NHSE spends. That is accepted.

↻ The loop closes: the return pays for the services that make the framework worth joining.

The loop under capability lots

More invoices per pathway, more levy collection points, per-capability assurance to fund, and an integrator role with no owner yet. The rule on this page, that an invoice should never need a team to calculate, is hardest to meet here. open The comparison is on the Lotting Choice page.

The critical assumption: the return leg

What the charge is called, the mechanism that brings the money back, the VAT treatment and the competition risk: none of the four is decided. Until they are, the return line stays an assumption, and the model says so wherever it appears.

Why suppliers pay

Three things come with NHSE that no other route offers: quality assurance and clinical validation, orders through the national app without building a route to permission of their own, and investment in the pathway change that makes home sampling the default.

The language

Any over-recovery is a contribution to development, the app for example. The return is a platform fee on throughput. It is never called anything else.

The discipline

Simple to run and transparent. If it takes a team to work out an invoice, the design is wrong.
