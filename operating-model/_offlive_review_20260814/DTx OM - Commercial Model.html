The Commercial Model.

Status. Agreed in principle from the sourcing strategy work. Firm enough to shape the model and to show the SLT, not settled enough to tender on. The one genuinely open piece, the mechanism for returning value to NHS England, is flagged where it sits.

How home self-sampling gets bought, how the money moves, and why NHS England sits in the middle of it. One end-to-end lot run by a framework provider. A levy that helps fund the system. A national ceiling price the market can beat locally. Payment for the activity done across the cycle rather than only for a completed test. Every chip on this page is a persona from the operating model, placed where they actually touch the commercial machine.

The deal in three lines

One lot. A levy that funds the system. A ceiling the market beats.

Suppliers bid to deliver the whole journey, request to result at home, as one lot. A framework provider runs it and takes a levy on what flows through, with a share coming back to NHS England to keep the system running. Commissioners buy at or below a national ceiling, and pay for the activity done, not only for the tests that complete. NHS England earns its place by assuring quality, holding the App gateway and shifting the pathway, which is what lets it charge at all.

The four moving parts

The levy, the price, the unit, and the reason NHS England can charge.

The picture above shows the flow. These are the four commercial mechanics underneath it, each agreed in principle and each with detail still to settle in the model.

The levy
A percentage of throughput, with a share back to NHS England.
The framework provider charges suppliers a levy on what flows through, in the order of one to two percent of revenue. The provider's costs come off, and a share of what is left comes back to NHS England to fund running the system. It is a contribution to keeping the framework alive and improving the App, not a cut taken from the transaction for its own sake, and because it is a percentage it only bites when a supplier is actually selling.
The price
A national ceiling the market can beat.
A national benchmark sets the most a commissioner pays for a given test. With competition and volume, a commissioner can negotiate below it through a mini-competition. The ceiling pulls the average cost down and stops runaway local pricing, without stopping a good local deal.
The unit
Paid for activity done, not only for tests completed.
Payment follows the activity across the cycle. A cycle fulfilled to seventy percent pays seventy percent. Whether a test completes depends on things the supplier does not control, the post and the app included, so completion stays a programme measure, not a stick. A volume-scaling reward shares the gain as activity grows, and recourse runs through performance standards in the contract rather than service credits, which just get priced back into the headline.
The reason it can charge
A differential offer a supplier cannot get elsewhere.
Three things justify the charge. Clinical validation and quality assurance run from inside NHS England, proving capillary sampling is non-inferior. Being on the framework gives automatic eligibility to be ordered through the NHS App. And NHS England invests in shifting the pathway so home testing becomes the default. Not a hundred percent differential, but enough to stand behind a levy.

The one that gates everything

The open question the model has to answer first.

Everything above holds in principle. One thing underneath it is genuinely unresolved, and it is load-bearing, so it is called out on its own rather than buried.

Open · resolve first
How value actually returns to NHS England.
The definition, the physical mechanism for the return, whether VAT applies, and whether it reads as anti-competitive. Everyone likes the headline of NHS England taking a share back, but the appetite drains fast if the mechanism is legally or financially awkward. Until there is a defensible position, the entire return line is an assumption sitting on top of the model. This is the first thing the deep-dive has to bottom out, because if it does not work the commercial shape changes.
