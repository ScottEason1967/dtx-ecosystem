Behavioural and relational dependencies 4 The people-side of the future-state shape. Architecture, contracts and procurement vehicles do not deliver outcomes on their own; sustained behaviour change among commissioners, clinicians, suppliers and NHSE is what makes them work in practice. Listed here so the behavioural dependencies sit at programme altitude alongside the structural ones.
Commissioner adoption of approved-list discipline
The architecture lives or dies on commissioners contracting through the approved list rather than reverting to "buy from the supplier we have used before". Adoption depends on commissioners changing established procurement habits. Without that behaviour change the list stays a register, not a working market. The matchmaker engagement model is the lever that converts the structural option into actual procurement behaviour.
Owners: NHSE pre-sales and engagement cell with commissioners.
Related: Engagement cell endorsement (SLT category).
Clinician trust at portfolio scale
Beta volumes prove the operating shape technically and the safety record at limited scale. Clinician trust in remote testing across pathways and regions has to be earned, not assumed, especially as the portfolio extends beyond HIV into PSA Active Surveillance, iron deficiency anaemia and Wave 1/2/3 tests. Sustained workforce engagement, peer advocacy from clinician partners and an observable safety record at MVP and beyond are what carry the operating model from defensible to adopted.
Owners: Clinical Safety Lead with workforce engagement and clinician peer network.
Supplier conformance behaviour beyond Beta
Suppliers conform to the published standard at Private Beta volumes because their NHSE relationship is dense and the assurance gaze is immediate. Sustaining standard-conforming behaviour at Public Beta volumes and into MVP and Wave 1 depends on the published standard, the assurance discipline and the removal protocol being credible enough to police drift without NHSE micromanaging each supplier. The supplier forum is one mechanism; the published standard plus the compelled-removal protocol is the other.
Owners: Independent Assurance Lead with supplier forum convenor.
Related: Compelled-removal protocol (Operational mechanism category).
NHSE convening discipline sustained across cycles
The model assumes NHSE convenes the system, sets standards and holds the assurance authority without becoming a commercial counterparty. The convening discipline has to survive SLT changes, funding cycles and political pressure to behave commercially. The future-state shape only holds if convening is treated as a sustained capability with named ownership and operating cadence, not a posture that depends on who is in the room at any given time.
Owners: Programme leadership with SLT.
Operating risks 12 Delivery dependencies the framework has to mitigate, not decisions to take.
Legal-direction lead time is six months end to end
A new or amended legal direction takes around six months from request to landing. The MI scope and data flows the direction covers must be locked before the request is issued; if scope shifts after issue, the request restarts and the next available answer is two cycles away. Treating the direction as a late-stage compliance step rather than a sequencing constraint blocks the programme for cycles at a time. Mitigation is deliberate MI-scope convergence before any direction request, and a one-shot discipline on what is submitted.
Owner: IG & Assurance SME with Commercial Operations, NHSE Commercial and SRO. Standing item on programme board.

Supplier off-boarding has no NHSE precedent
The approved list needs a defined process for removing a supplier that fails standards, exits the market or is replaced. There is no precedent elsewhere in NHSE the platform can lift wholesale. Compelled removal is the enforcement mechanism in the interim shape (see Compelled removal position paper). Operational mechanics (notice periods, evidence thresholds, appeals, patient continuity, sample-in-flight handling, residual contractual obligations) all need to be designed. Without it the platform locks in to first-mover suppliers by default.
Owner: Commercial Operations with NHSE Commercial and Head of Operations. Cross-reference to ASSURE off-boarding trigger criteria.

Pricing model directionAnswered in principle
Ceiling-price approach for the Open Framework: maximum prices set nationally, commissioners run their own competitions and price down based on volume. Three-tier structure underneath the cap (mobilisation fee, activity on cost per valid result, volume floor). Flat per-kit pricing rejected through RFI and supplier forums; subscription rejected at strategic level. Final activity-unit position is A9 in the recommendation paper. Exploratory, not decided: a second one per cent fee, in addition to the supplier-paid framework levy, to recover NHS App costs and run the framework as a small profit centre. Not validated and not decided. Sourcing strategy outcome (in principle): cost per activity, not per completed test; a national ceiling that commissioners beat locally; recourse through KPIs rather than service credits.
