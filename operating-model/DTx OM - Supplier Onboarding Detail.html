Supplier onboarding: the detail.

Status. Rebuilt to the sourcing strategy position agreed in principle: a preferred single end-to-end lot (being stress-tested against a multi-lot approach), a two-tier assurance model, and capability gates opening on a published roadmap. Decided in principle, not settled design. The open items are marked at the foot of the page, and the detailed assurance stages still need iteration with the programme team across IG, Clinical Safety, Commercial and Platform.
Scope. This page is HomeTest only: onboarding for the home self-sampling framework.

The applicant journey step by step, where each requirement sits across the two tiers, the design rules the framework holds itself to, and the open items. The overview lives on the main page.

The journey, gate by gate

The process is published, automated where it can be, with human review where judgement is needed.

Before anyone engages, a supplier can read everything, self-assess and decide whether to proceed. The framework's assurance runs in the amber-bordered stages.

1
Discovery
The criteria for both layers and the category roadmap are published. A supplier reads the bar and the timetable before spending anything.
2
Self-assessment
Structured self-assessment against the published pack, using the template artefacts. No engagement needed to reach a view.
3
Apply at a gate
An application is made against an open gate, declaring the categories sought and the evidence held.
4
Tier 1 approval
Tier 1 runs once: automated triage on completeness, human review where judgement is needed. Time-boxed, with a published turnaround.Framework entry
5
Tier 2 approval
Per-category evidence is judged at Tier 2. The clinician panel takes the clinical bar; automated conformance takes the technical one. Under capability lots this would run against a lot-specific pack, not yet designed.Category gate
6
Integrate and witness
API and journey conformance against the versioned specifications, then a witnessed clinical journey where a human needs to watch. Go-live sign-off ends it.Go live
7
Later gates
Adding a category repeats Tier 2 only, at any open gate. Tier 1 approval is confirmed annually and never repeated. Exit runs to a defined offboarding process.
Where each requirement sits

Five dimensions, split across the two tiers.

The dimensions have not changed. What has changed is where each one sits, so nothing is asked twice and nothing clinically decisive hides inside a company-level pass. Most of this is captured on a single NHS instrument, the Master SCAL. Company assurance sits on its company tab, completed once. Platform, information governance and clinical-safety conformance sit on its service tab, per category. What the SCAL does not carry, the framework adds around it: the commercial standing at Tier 1, and the diagnostic evidence at Tier 2.

Seven design principles

The rules the design follows.

Each principle is load-bearing. The whole framework weakens if any one of them is dropped in the build.

Principle 01
Two tiers, kept separate.
Company assurance once per supplier. Test and capability assurance once per category. Keeping them separate is what stops re-assurance creeping in at either level.
Principle 02
Criteria are published, not gated.
Criteria and roadmap live in the open. A supplier reads, self-assesses and decides before any engagement. Publication exposes the design to challenge, which improves it.
Principle 03
Automate the routine checks.
Completeness, conformance and technical proof run without a human. People take the grey areas: clinical safety, information governance, the borderline calls. Where that line falls sets the cost and the queue.
Principle 04
The roadmap is the fairness mechanism.
Gates and categories publish far enough ahead that any capable laboratory can get ready. A gate that opens without lead time is just a queue.
Principle 05
Assess fitness rather than ration entry.
Any qualified provider means the answer to capacity pressure is automation and better process, not barriers. A queue is a legal risk.
Principle 06
Every artefact exists once.
DPIA template, data sharing template, privacy wording, safety case starter, integration guide. One version of each, held centrally, inherited everywhere.
Principle 07
Supplier experience is a design input.
The journey is designed from the supplier's side first. If a capable supplier cannot find the bar, cannot self-assess or cannot apply without a private conversation, the design has failed.
The open items

What is still open.

Agreed in principle does not mean settled. These are the items still moving.

Open 01
The stages in detail, and how much automates.
The straw-man assurance process is being recirculated with a view on exactly what can run without a human. The dividing line between automated conformance and human judgement sets the cost, the queue and what the levy has to fund.
Open 02
One door or two.
Whether framework entry and category accreditation present to a supplier as one journey or two, and how the centre's assurance and the framework provider's process mesh without duplicating either.
Open 03
The test standard itself.
What the per-category bar actually cites: accreditation scope, the recognised standards, and a route for capable laboratories that do not yet hold them. Being defined with the clinical and laboratory community.
Open 04
Cadence and lead time.
Roughly six-monthly is the working rhythm. The exact reopening cadence, and how far ahead the roadmap must publish so accreditation booking times do not swallow the window, are still to be set.
Open 05
Live before fully plugged in.
Whether a supplier can start delivering ahead of full platform integration, and what that would do to the eligibility levers that make framework membership worth having.
Open 06
The roadmap itself.
The prioritisation mechanism is designed, but the first published roadmap of categories and gate dates has not been agreed. Suppliers and consortium builders plan against it; it is the gating open action for this page.
