Lifecycle frame
WHEN

Two loops, four beats

The rhythm by which the model itself gets better, adapt, implement, review, learn

There are two loops in the operating model, running at the same time on different clocks. The inner loop is the rhythm of doing, the five-stage operational cycle (Shape, Assure, Contract, Fulfil, Measure). The outer loop is the slow turn by which the model itself gets better, adapt the standards and framework, implement the changes, review what they produce, learn the patterns, then adapt again. Plan, Do, Check, Act, the continuous-improvement cycle, applied to the model itself. The model is the relationship between the two loops.

A note on what this page shows. The lifecycle below is the future-state operating shape, not a frozen TOM. What is settled, what is in flight and what is open today surface stage by stage in the Decided / In Progress / Open Decision blocks on each stage page, and in full on Status. The shape itself reflects the future-state work the sponsor and SLT have asked for; the items inside it are live programme positions, not commitments.

The inner loop · The cadence
The rhythm of doing
The repeating beat of the work itself. Its shape is the business: for the NHS DTx platform that is supplier admission cycles, governance reviews, contract management, service delivery and operational measurement. The loop you can feel; the one with a calendar.
On this model: the inner loop is run by the platform and the suppliers. NHSE convenes it; it is the operational pentagon below.
The outer loop · The renewal
The loop of improving
The slow turn by which the model gets better: adapt → implement → review → learn. Plan, Do, Check, Act. What each cycle reveals feeds back and changes the next. The part that makes the model alive rather than fixed.
On this model: the outer loop is where NHSE earns its keep. Quiet today; gets loud as configurations multiply and patterns surface.
↻ The two loops, in picture

The outer loop is drawn around the pentagon as a slow turning halo with four beats, adapt, implement, review, learn, at the compass points. The inner loop is the pentagon itself. Their relative speeds are deliberate: the inner turns several times per outer turn.

HealthStore · per-stage lifecycle view

Open HealthStore product page →

COPD Alpha is the lead use case. Three Business Cases (BC-27512, BC-28519, BC-28891) form the capital stack. NICE EVA conditional admission is the assurance entry. ~10 month delivery window. Class IIa entry condition. Content surfaced from the DPSP HealthStore folder.

SHAPE

What this stage does. Establishes the HealthStore proposition as a national B2B2C clinical activation layer for scaling NICE-assessed digital therapeutics through the NHS App, with COPD selected as the exemplar condition for Alpha.

Active programme work. Discovery concluded January 2026 with COPD confirmed as lead use case following Respiratory selection by IRLSS / Health Tech Advisory Board (Sep 2025). CVD (hypertension / Cardiac Rehab) confirmed as fast-follower with Alpha-shaped prototyping from Jun/Jul 2026. MSK time-boxed design (Sep–Dec 2026) optional.

In flight. Commissioner Store (B2B) design and prototyping, roadshow-ready by Jul 2026, aligned to commissioning workflows and remuneration strategy. Wearables integration patterns being designed to inform DHSC/NHSE wearables policy direction targeted for August 2026.

Open. Scaling methodology beyond COPD and growth strategy for FY26/27 onwards. Whether HealthStore is positioned as distributor or intermediary platform (legal review pending).

ASSURE

What this stage does. Sets minimum entry standards (NICE HTG recommendation or EVA conditional admission, MHRA Class IIa minimum, full DTAC compliance, NHS App integration standards). Designs the central assurance approach as integrator across NICE, MHRA, DTAC and Innovator Passport.

Active programme work. COPD Alpha apps in scope are HTG736 and HTG718. Beta pipeline extends to Cardiac Rehab HTG761, MSK HTG712/766 and dCBTi HTG624. By September 2026, programme expects to be able to assert whether vendors and NHS organisations can meet minimum standards.

In flight. NICE engagement (29 April 2026) confirmed support for HealthStore using NICE-recommended / EVA-conditional technologies, with evidence-generation requirements as a potential onboarding condition. Actual product usage data identified as “gold dust” for evidence generation.

Open. Dependency on Innovator Passport programme for central document repository and certification route. Risk that NHS organisations assume HealthStore entry removes need for local assurance. Visibility of NICE annual supplier evidence monitoring into HealthStore.

CONTRACT

What this stage does. Manages the three-business-case capital approval stack and supplier engagement: BC-27512 (revenue, phases 0–1), BC-28519 (capital mobilisation Feb–Mar 2026), BC-28891 (FY26/27 Alpha + Beta delivery).

Active programme work. BC-28891 funds COPD Alpha build + NHS App integration, COPD Beta delivery, CVD (hypertension) prototyping, wearables integration and Commissioner Store demonstrator. Contracting route is a new Call-Off and SOW with the lead delivery supplier, with partnership agreements / call-offs for third-party DTx suppliers and device partners.

In flight. Supplier ways-of-working established with Alpha suppliers (April–May 2026 kick-off) on 2-week NHSE sprints, fortnightly SLT end-of-sprint reviews. Supplier onboarding under consistent requirements for integration, assurance and data sharing.

Open. Commissioner remuneration / tariff model and commercial model for Commissioner Store. ACE commercial framework transition triggered the BC-28519 short mobilisation phase.

FULFIL

What this stage does. Delivers the COPD Alpha (Apr–Sep 2026) and Beta (Sep 2026–Mar 2027), proving NHS App integration patterns, service wrap and operational viability before national scale.

Active programme work. Alpha will produce a validated COPD service & pathway design, working prototype integration with two suppliers, governance/safety/IG foundations, evidence pack and a clear go/no-go to Beta decision. Integration uses app-to-app handoff from NHS App into existing COPD DTx suppliers using NHS login SSO where possible, with pilot-cohort access limits.

In flight. COPD Alpha mobilised April 2026 with Alpha suppliers under co-design ways of working. CVD Alpha-shaped prototyping initiating Jun/Jul 2026. Wearables pattern work underway to inform August 2026 policy direction.

Open. Beta mobilisation scope and size. Whether the Commissioner Store and HealthStore platform itself moves from prototype to functional service inside FY26/27 (explicitly out of scope for Alpha).

MEASURE

What this stage does. Establishes consistent KPI and outcomes reporting across suppliers, supports NICE evidence generation during EVA periods, and underpins commissioner confidence with population-level evaluation.

Active programme work. Outcomes framework drafted Dec 2025. HealthStore Benefits Tree, HMT Green Book Benefits and MI Benefits Mapping for ROI in development. myCOPD uptake and health economic impact modelling using existing supplier evaluation data.

In flight. Platform evaluation measures being defined. Supplier MI metrics being collected. NICE discussion raised possibility of commissioner-facing evidence-status visibility.

Open. Whether HealthStore can capture actual usage and link via SDE to wider NHS datasets for evaluation (NICE: “gold dust”). Whether NICE annual supplier evidence-monitoring scores can flow into HealthStore.

Per-product detail

HealthSync · per-stage lifecycle view

Open HealthSync product page →

HealthSync is a PHR-anchored data-integration capability (Apple HealthKit / Google Health Connect ingest into the NHS App via Patient Data Manager), not a diagnostic device service. Conventional stages map imperfectly, CONTRACT in particular is thin because the model deliberately avoids hundreds of bilateral device contracts in favour of two platform integrations.

BP monitoring is the lead use case. East Lindsey PoC has been done. Wearables Workshop 1 Definition is the densest single primary source.

SHAPE

What this stage does. Frames the wearables/biosensors proposition as a PHR-anchored capability layer, defining nomenclature (bio-measurement → biometric sensors → connected biosensors → wearables), positioning value across the care continuum, and prioritising clinical domain.

Active programme work. Cardiovascular Disease set as priority domain, starting with Hypertension Management as the lead use case (10YP commitment: remote CVD monitoring via wearables in NHS App standard by 2028). Discovery exam question: “What is the benefit for me, if I share my wearable information with the NHS?”

In flight. BP use case prioritisation, 14 candidate BP use cases scored against viability conditions. Diagnosed-hypertension monitoring and case-finding scoring highest. National Wearables Strategy targeted for Autumn 2026.

Open. Definition scope, whether “wearables” is too narrow and should expand to “connected devices”. Device-led vs health-outcome-led entry. Whether Care Tech is in scope.

ASSURE

What this stage does. Resolves the IG, regulatory and clinical-safety architecture for HealthKit/Health Connect data flowing into and out of the NHS App, covering consent, persistence, write-back to clinical record, and policy/research re-use.

Active programme work. Four-quadrant IG model mapped (data use in native NHS App; processing via internal API; sending to clinical record; use for policy/research). Existing platform-partner T&Cs cover local rendering only. NHS-specific T&Cs required for clinical record write-back, policy or research use.

In flight. Policy review of wearable data use, storage and persistence raised with policy colleagues. Active barriers: IG/liability, workforce trust/clinical liability, device regulation, IP ownership for AI development on wearables data.

Open. Whether persisted records and write-back require uplift to NHS App T&Cs or a separate set. Legal/DSA basis for porting clinician-controlled record data via patient-mediated channels. Sensor-accuracy verification where platform partners do not validate. AI Health Coach regulation.

CONTRACT

What this stage does. Defines the commercial shape of two platform-partner integrations (rather than hundreds of bilateral device contracts) plus consequential commercial model for clinical services consuming the data.

Active programme work. Strategic rationale paper authored arguing two integrations with the two smartphone health-data aggregators dominates alternatives on coverage, speed-to-value (~6–12 months) and ongoing cost. Live discussions ongoing with prominent consumer wearable companies.

In flight. Commercial barriers flagged as top-tier alongside IG and workforce. British Heart Foundation engagement flagged for grant-funded CVD innovation pipeline.

Open. Thin / Reserved pending HealthSync programme detail, no NHS Standard Contract artefacts found in primary source. Build/buy/integrate decision for AI Health Coach unresolved. Funding-incentive scheme design and VfM evaluation methodology not yet defined.

FULFIL

What this stage does. Builds the middleware (Patient Data Manager + new “NHS Data Sync” component) that brokers data between platform partners’ on-device health stores and NHS internal systems, and surfaces it in the NHS App.

Active programme work. Successful PoC completed for BP reading input via three routes, manual entry in NHS App, automated via platform partners’ health stores, and direct device-provider integration. Personal Health Profile being designed in NHS App by Core Infrastructure, fed by wearable devices, in collaboration with PPS.

In flight. Work to connect readings to PDM storage ongoing. Data-flow architecture defined across PDM, Clinical Record, NHS App, platform partners’ health stores, NHS Data Sync, and third-party apps.

Open. Build of NHS middleware to control data flow to/from platform partners. De-duplication/data hierarchy design. Outward data-sharing capability. Education/comms programme on how data is stored and secured. Whether the platform partners’ record-sharing specification meets NHS requirements in full.

MEASURE

What this stage does. Establishes the clinical and economic evaluation framework for whether wearable-derived data produces a material, marginal difference in outcomes, and how benefit is sustained.

Active programme work. East Lindsey PoC (with Lincolnshire ICB) delivered, targeting 10% reduction in hospital admissions among residents in temporary housing across three GP practices, modelled at £240k/year savings to the ICS. Predates HealthSync’s HealthKit/Health Connect framing, relevant as wearables-in-pathway evidence, not as PHR-integration evidence.

In flight. CVD Modern Service Framework clinical engagement exercise generating insights on emerging needs and technologies. Logic model defined from devices through to “improving healthcare outcomes”.

Open. Who evaluates clinical and economic outcomes. Whether wearables make a demonstrable, material, marginal difference. How benefits are sustained over time. Grid/matrix mapping use cases against barriers and operating-model changes proposed but not yet drafted. Real-world evidence base, international case studies and partnerships flagged as needed but not yet established.
