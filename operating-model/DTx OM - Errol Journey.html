The credibility unlock
The smartwatch catches what Errol can't.
Errol feels recovered and wants to get on with his life. He isn't watching his own heart, and he wouldn't know what to watch for. The smartwatch is the always-on sensor doing exactly that: resting and recovery heart rate, exertion tolerance, nocturnal rhythm. In the months after a cardiac event, when the risk of arrhythmia is real, it spots a signal he didn't even know to look for.
Why one operating model
He onboards once. Consents once. One record. One care team view.
Across the journey his identity, his consent, his clinical context, his data routing and his clinical safety governance carry across every workstream boundary. He doesn't re-onboard at any of them. That is what makes this one operating model rather than three products glued together.

The catch · the watch sees what he can't

Errol feels fine. The smartwatch sees a run of irregular overnight rhythm and a resting heart rate that will not settle. In a patient recovering from a heart attack, that is a pattern nobody wants to miss.

I didn't feel a thing. A bit tired maybe, but I'm fifty-six and I'd just had a heart attack, so of course I was tired. My rehab nurse called. Said she'd been watching the watch data and wanted to take a closer look.

Errol
What the model is doing underneath
Irregular overnight rhythm and an unsettled resting heart rate are recognised early warnings of arrhythmia in the months after a cardiac event, and they show up in passive wearable data before the patient feels them. The HealthSync configuration carries threshold and pattern rules per use case, not a single cut-off but “rhythm irregularity over X nights combined with a resting heart-rate shift of more than Y”. Those rules sit in the operational layer. When triggered, the signal routes to the responsible clinical pathway, which is why Errol's rehab team responds by bringing his review forward and ordering bloods. The watch signal is the prompt, the bloods and the clinical review are the confirmation. The catch is the secondary signal the patient isn't looking at. The wearable is doing the work Errol couldn't.
👥
What the care team sees
Alert in the dashboard with the rhythm pattern shown. The rehab team sees it against his recovery baseline, decides it is worth a closer look, and calls him in.

Diagnostic · the test that confirms what the data suggested

A blood test kit arrives on the doorstep two days later. Lipids, kidney function, HbA1c: the things you check to keep a patient recovering from a heart attack on the right side of a second one.

It came in the post. Bit of a finger-prick, post it back, two days later the result was sitting in the NHS App right under my heart-rate trend. Same app. Same record. I didn't set anything up.

Errol
What the model is doing underneath
The HomeTest workstream activates without re-onboarding Errol. The same NHS App handles the kit dispatch, the instructions, the return tracking and the result, not a separate app. NHS Notify carries every comms touchpoint under the same identity. The result lands in the same patient record as his recovery data because the IG framework treats him as one patient across workstreams, not as a different patient per product.

Prescription · the rehab programme, delivered to his door

The rehab team optimises his medication and prescribes his cardiac rehabilitation as a digital therapeutic, myHeart. By the time Errol is off the call it is sitting in his NHS App waiting to be started.

She said the tablets would do their bit, but the app was my rehab: guided exercise sessions I could do at home, the education, someone keeping an eye on how I was getting on. By the time I'd made another cup of tea it was in the App. Same NHS Login. Tap, in.

Errol
What the model is doing underneath
A cardiac rehabilitation digital therapeutic from the HealthStore catalogue enters Errol's care. It earned its place the HealthStore way: a NICE Health Tech Guidance assessment for cardiac rehab, DTAC compliance and MHRA Class IIa device status. DCB0129 sits with the admitted supplier; DCB0160 sits with the deploying NHS organisation. A split, not a joint. The Service Wrapper at Foundation level holds Errol between clinical touchpoints with engagement nudges and signposting. Errol never browses a marketplace: his commissioner curated the product onto the Commissioner Store, and his clinician prescribed this one.

What Errol's story shows

Three workstreams, one operating model.
Three changes, for three audiences.

Three separate operating models would have forced Errol to onboard three times, sign three IG agreements, exist as three patients to the NHS. Three care teams would have to reconcile three views of him. The operating model exists so that none of that happens, and so that the things that do happen are the right things at the right time.

For Errol
A crisis that never happened.
Recovery on track. Rehab completed. No readmission. His reviews are mostly virtual. He onboarded once and consented once for a journey that crossed three workstreams. He doesn't experience three products. He experiences one NHS App that has things in it.
For the care team
Intervention before crisis.
The wearable signal arrived weeks before the clinical event would have. The diagnostic confirmed it. The therapeutic supported the response. Three signal streams in one record, not three systems to reconcile. Clinical time freed for the patients who genuinely need to be in clinic.
For the system
Pathway value, demonstrated.
The wearable carried the routine monitoring, so the review appointments that used to watch a recovering patient stopped being needed. The bloods came to his door instead of a clinic slot. The rehab ran at home instead of a hospital gym slot. And the cardiac readmission that never happened is the largest saved cost of all. Value released at the pathway level, not the test line, and the case for the next pathway writes itself from the evidence this one generated.
One person. Three signal streams. One record. Intervention before the crisis, not after it.
