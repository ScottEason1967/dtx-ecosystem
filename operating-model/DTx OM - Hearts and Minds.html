The operating model in human voice

The cast.
Thirteen stories.

Operating models live or die on whether the people inside them want them to work: the patients who use the service, the commissioners who buy it, the clinicians who prescribe it, and the suppliers who build it. Each group is here, in their own voice, each grounded in one person you can hold in your head. Thirteen stories across four audiences, one operating model.

Why hearts and minds
Architecture wins arguments. Stories win adoption.
An operating model lives or dies on whether the people inside it want it to work. The architecture pages on this site explain how the model is meant to operate. These stories explain why it matters to the audiences whose buy-in it needs: the patients who use the service, the commissioners who buy it, the clinicians who prescribe it, and the suppliers who build it. Each follows one named person through the operating model in their own voice, with their stakes, and with the failure modes the model is designed to prevent. The cards below group by audience.

How to use these stories
Talking to a patient group
Lead with Errol. The crisis that never happened. Three workstreams, one patient experience.
Talking to an ICB
Lead with Sarah. Easy buying as a real, named experience. Six weeks not eighteen months.
Talking to a supplier
Lead with Marcus. The framework as the antidote to pilot purgatory. NICE EVA conditional admission as a real route in.
Talking to a clinician
Lead with Ravi. Sceptic to advocate. The clinical evidence the conditional admission lets you trust enough to prescribe.
Talking about health inequalities
Lead with Sean. The cohorts every pathway fails the same way, and the second scaling axis that serves them all at once. See also the Horizontal Cohorts lane.
