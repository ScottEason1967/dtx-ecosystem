A patient's story through HealthStore, the prescribed digital therapeutic

A prescription that lives in her pocket,
and the service that keeps her on it.

Pauline is 64, has moderate COPD and was prescribed a digital therapeutic by her respiratory consultant. She does the breathing exercises in the morning, then takes the dog out. This is the prescribed-DTx journey, told in five acts: the consultant, the app, the programme, the moment it flags something, and what the pathway gets back.

The patient North Star
Stay well enough to keep her Sundays
Pauline is not trying to use an app. She is trying to keep out of hospital over the winter, keep her breath for the dog walk and be fit for three grandchildren most Sundays. The digital therapeutic only matters to her if it fits into that morning routine and if someone notices when things slide.
What the model has to get right
The prescribed pattern, end to end
HealthStore's lead configuration is COPD supported self-management: roughly 80% of the COPD population, which the model expects to manage with digital-therapeutic-led care inside a service wrapper, escalating to clinical input when the therapeutic surfaces a concern. Ravi prescribes from the National App Formulary, Daniel buys the episode for the ICB, the supplier delivers the programme, and the NHS App is where all of it shows up for Pauline. If any link in that chain is loose, engagement is theatre and the value case collapses.

What the model is doing underneath
The journey starts in Ravi's clinic, not on a home screen. A prescribed digital therapeutic is a clinical decision: the consultant confirms Pauline sits in the supported self-management tier, checks the therapeutic covers the OPTIMISE processes her care plan needs (pulmonary rehabilitation, education and self-management, physical activity, psychosocial support), and prescribes it from the National App Formulary. That formulary is the HealthStore equivalent of the accredited register: the supplier has already passed assurance, clinical safety and conformance before Ravi ever sees the product. The prescription is recorded against Pauline's NHS record, with the NHS as custodian.
The old way
A leaflet about pulmonary rehab classes twelve miles away, a referral with a waiting list, and a consultant hoping she turned up. Digital tools, if mentioned at all, were a suggestion at the door rather than part of the plan.
👥
What the care team sees
Ravi sees a prescription like any other on her record: what was prescribed, when, for how long, and against which part of her care plan. The respiratory nurse sees the same thing without having to ask.

What the model is doing underneath
The four commercial principles do their work here: a third-party digital therapeutic, surfaced inside the NHS App, under NHS Login, with the NHS holding the record. Pauline does not download a separate product, create a supplier account or hand her details to a company she has never heard of. The prescription unlocks the therapeutic inside the app her daughter set up. The onboarding is the supplier's, delivered inside the NHS front door, and consent for the data flows is captured once, in plain language. If she cannot get in on the first evening, the model has already failed her.
The old way
Download this, register there, verify your email, set a password, then find the code the clinic gave you. Half the cohort never got past the second screen and nobody knew.
👥
What the care team sees
Ravi and the respiratory nurse can see that she activated the programme. Activation is the first engagement signal the model records and the first place a non-starter shows up as an exception rather than a silence.

What the model is doing underneath
This is the long middle of the episode and the part that decides whether the value case is real. The therapeutic runs guided pulmonary rehab, breathing tracking and symptom logging. Around it sits the service wrapper the model treats as part of the product: self-management education, motivational support, signposting and escalation, delivered as nudges and milestone check-ins across the episode. The wrapper is what keeps a wary 64-year-old on the programme between clinical touchpoints. Engagement is measured in ways that are clinically meaningful and hard to game: completed sessions, logged symptoms, breathing trends, not app opens. That measurement is what the marketplace payments mechanism being designed in Alpha will ultimately rest on, and the model keeps it honest for that reason.
The old way
Twelve weeks of rehab classes if she could get there, then nothing. Self-management meant a booklet. Whether she was doing any of it was a question asked once a year in clinic.
👥
What the care team sees
The respiratory team sees adherence and symptom trends across the cohort, not just for Pauline. The wrapper handles the routine encouragement so clinical time is spent on the patients whose trend has turned.

What the model is doing underneath
The escalation route is where the prescribed pattern earns its place. The therapeutic surfaces a concern from the trend it can see: symptom scores rising, breathing measures falling, a pattern the care plan defined as a trigger. That becomes a patient-initiated or clinician-initiated escalation through the model's PI/CI route, into the respiratory team with the data attached. Pauline can also raise it herself from inside the app. Either way the escalation lands as a structured event in the NHS record. The therapeutic does not diagnose or treat the exacerbation; it makes sure a clinician sees it early.
The old way
Pauline waits it out, because she has had bad weeks before. By the time she calls 111 she is in the pattern that ends in an admission.
👥
What the care team sees
The nurse gets the trend, the trigger and the prescription context in one view. The consultant sees whether the escalation changed the plan. The admission that did not happen is the outcome the ICB is paying for.

What the model is doing underneath
The episode ends with data returning to the people who need it. Outcome capture sits with the clinical team but draws on Pauline's engagement and self-reported measures: adherence, symptom trajectory, breathing trends, escalations and what followed. That is the pathway-level value case Daniel needs to keep buying HealthStore COPD for the ICB, and the evidence the supplier needs to stay on the formulary. The model is strict on two things: engagement must be real and measured, and outcomes must be honest. Pauline is the load-bearing actor in that case. If the model does not serve her, there is no case.
The old way
Outcome was whether she was readmitted, learned after the fact from hospital activity data. Nothing connected the rehab she did, or did not do, to what happened next.
👥
What the care team sees
The respiratory team can show what the programme did across its cohort, patient by patient and in aggregate. The commissioner sees the same numbers. Nobody is arguing from anecdote.
Pauline's win

A treatment she actually does, a team that sees her slipping before she does, and a winter at home.

Pauline's story is the prescribed-DTx pattern working as designed. The consultant carries the clinical decision. The NHS App carries the front door and the record. The supplier carries the programme and the wrapper. The commissioner carries the episode. What holds it together is that her engagement is real and her outcomes are captured honestly, because that is the currency the whole HealthStore model trades in. Daniel and Ravi tell the same story from the other two seats.

The money in this story

Pauline pays nothing. The digital therapeutic is prescribed and free at the point of use, surfaced inside the NHS App. Daniel's ICB buys the COPD episode for the supported self-management cohort, in principle on pathway-level terms rather than per download, which is why the model insists that engagement is measured in a way that cannot be gamed and that outcomes are captured honestly.

The marketplace payments mechanism that settles how the supplier is paid is being designed in Alpha. Whatever it lands on, what has to be true at Pauline's level is that the engagement and the outcomes are real.

Commercial positions are agreed in principle and remain subject to the live procurement. See Money Loop and How Commissioners Buy.
